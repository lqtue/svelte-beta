# SOP Craft Notes — Learning From the EALAC Model

Reverse-engineered from `SoP Columbia (1).pdf` (Do, East Asian Languages and Cultures, PhD application 2025). ~1,000 words, four paragraphs, wildly uneven by design.

Applies to both the Cornell HAUD SOP (`cornell-application-plan.md` §8) and the Columbia GSAPP SOP (`gsapp-application-plan.md`).

---

## 1. Why it works

It never argues that the applicant is good. It **demonstrates competence by performing it** — the reader watches someone do close reading on a real archival find, and concludes on their own that this person can do the work.

That is the whole trick. Everything below is in service of it.

---

## 2. The architecture

| Para | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~150 | Claim + field correction |
| 2 | **~400** | **The specimen** — one concrete find, read closely, escalating to theory |
| 3 | ~180 | Training, framed strictly as method-provenance |
| 4 | ~250 | Fit — named people, each tied to a named gap |

**The specimen paragraph is 40% of the document.** That proportion is the single most important thing to copy. Most applicants spend 40% on biography and 10% on evidence, and it reads as a CV in prose.

## 3. The six transferable moves

### Move 1 — The field-correction opening

Three beats, no wasted words:

> "**Commonly classified as** resulting from the influence of French symbolism… **Nevertheless, it has been studied mainly as** an ideological phase of later French colonialism… **This limited categorization elides** its complex and lasting poetic legacies…"

*What the field says → what the field has therefore missed → what I will do about it.*

It positions against existing scholarship without attacking a single scholar by name. Note "elides" — the literature isn't wrong, it's incomplete. That's the right register.

**Your version.** The received account of Saigon's development is the colonial core, Chợ Lớn, and the post-1975 expansion. District 4 appears in it as port infrastructure, or not at all. The elision is the peninsula itself — and, more sharply, that the elision is *in the sources*, not just in the scholarship. That is a stronger version of this move than the model's, because your gap is documentary rather than interpretive.

### Move 2 — The specimen (the engine)

The applicant does not summarise their archive. They produce **one thing**, and it is tiny:

> a 1939 article in *Tao Đàn tạp chí* by Hoài Thanh, complaining that the New Poets misuse the possessive **của** in imitation of French **de** — "đại dương **của** thương nhớ" instead of the classical "bể ái tình"

**One preposition.** From that single word, the paragraph reaches Russian Formalism, classical poetics, and creolization theory. And it establishes, without a word of self-description: they were in the archive; they read 1939 Vietnamese criticism; they read Proust in French; they notice things at the level of the morpheme.

**The rule: the specimen must be small enough to quote and large enough to carry the thesis.**

**Your version — and the real work ahead.** You do not have this yet, and it cannot be invented. You need **one named thing on one dated sheet**: a wharf, an alley, a canal, a parcel, a toponym. Something you can name, date, quote from the source, and then show being drawn, mis-drawn, or refused across regimes.

Shape it should take:

> On the 1882 *Plan Cadastral de la ville de Saigon*, [named feature] is drawn as [X]. In the [1968 sheet / KH-9 frame of 4 November 1972], the same ground carries [Y]. The cadastral names every parcel across the river; on the peninsula it names [nothing / only the wharves]. What the surveyor declined to record is now [Khánh Hội, the densest ward in Vietnam].

Where to hunt for it, in order:
1. **Your own 366 label pins on the 1882 cadastral.** You have already read that sheet more closely than anyone. What is on the peninsula, and what is missing from it?
2. **The 1863 *Plan du port de Saigon* and 1864 *Saigon Port Plan*** — port-specific sheets that must show the peninsula. What do they show *behind* the wharf line?
3. **The 1959 *Đô thành Sài Gòn*** (still `draft`) — the hinge year.
4. **Gallica / *Journal Officiel*** — a single concession, decree, or public-works notice naming the ground. A named legal instrument beats a general observation.

One found specimen is worth more to this application than fifty more georeferenced maps.

### Move 3 — Theory arrives late, and only through the specimen

Shklovsky is never introduced. Defamiliarization appears *because* it explains why Hoài Thanh's complaint is actually evidence of success. Shih and Lionnet appear at the end of the paragraph, after the reading has earned them.

**Never open with theory.** Your `docs/theory.md` Lefebvre triad is genuinely load-bearing — *espace conçu* is the drawn city, *espace vécu* is the inhabited one — but it should surface **after** the specimen, in one or two sentences, as the name for what the reader has already been shown. Not as a framework announced in advance.

### Move 4 — The self-limiting sentence

> "As creolization is a context-specific concept, I am not proposing a project that turns local discourses into abstract universals."

They name the obvious objection and disarm it. This is the clearest maturity signal in the document — it says *I know what's wrong with projects like mine.*

**Your version.** Two candidates, pick one:
- *Absence of evidence.* A blank on a map is not proof of an empty ground; it may be a surveyor's scope, not a refusal. Say that you are reading omissions **against** press, gazette, and photographic evidence precisely so that silence is corroborated rather than assumed. This is the objection a historian will raise first, and raising it yourself is worth more than the paragraph it costs.
- *Technology.* You are not proposing that georeferencing settles historical questions. The pipeline produces a comparable frame; the argument is made from sources. Say it in one sentence and the "GIS person applying to a history program" worry dies.

Take the first. It is the sharper objection and the one Chi is likelier to have.

### Move 5 — Biography strictly as method-provenance

St. John's is mentioned **only** because it explains where close reading came from, including the detail that its pedagogy discourages secondary sources. Dartmouth is mentioned for literary theory. The year of French at Lyon is mentioned because the project requires French.

Every biographical item earns its place by supplying a capability the project needs. No prestige, no passion, no origin story.

**Your version.** MPP → *who benefited from each drawing* as a working question. VMA → the georeferenced baseline and the pipeline. VnExpress → the officials and the primary documentation. `greenest-ward` → quantitative spatial analysis at national scale, submitted. **The year in District 4 → fieldwork that begins inside the neighbourhood.** One clause each. Nothing about wanting to help the city.

### Move 6 — Fit as gap-filling, not admiration

Four names, each attached to something the applicant **cannot do alone**: Phan for philology and chữ Nôm, Miller (already used) for Vietnamese scholarship, Nguyen for French Indochina history, Gourgouris for comparative poetics. Plus an institute, named, with the specific track.

The formula is *"X's work on [specific thing] will be indispensable to [specific part of my project]."* Never "I admire X's work."

**Your version.** Chi → Saigon, propaganda, incremental housing; she is the reason the project is at Cornell. Echols → the RVN documents and the 1968 sheet, named. Akcan / González Pendás → one clause. SEAP → language and fieldwork. And be explicit about what you lack: **you are not trained as an architectural historian.** Name the training you need. That converts your weakest point into a reason to admit you.

## 4. What not to copy

- **The closing sentence.** "…will serve as a great base for me to launch my professional academic career" is filler, and it is the weakest line in the document. End on the argument, not on your career.
- **No feasibility beat.** A literature SOP doesn't need one — the archive is text and the method is reading. **Yours does.** Five source types across four regimes has to be shown to be doable, and you are the person who can show it, because four of the five are already in hand.
- **No stakes beyond the discipline.** Acceptable there. For HAUD, one line on why this district matters beyond itself is worth having — but as historiography (*what reading maps as instruments changes about how Saigon has been written*), never as policy.
- **Length calibration.** That SOP runs ~1,000 words with no stated cap. **Cornell's cap is 1,000 words and it is a real cap.** Your para-2 equivalent has to do more work in the same space, which means the specimen must be chosen ruthlessly — one, not three.

## 5. Your revised architecture

Replaces the seven-paragraph outline in `cornell-application-plan.md` §8. Four paragraphs, deliberately uneven, 1,000 words hard.

| Para | Words | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Claim + correction** | ~140 | District 4 as the object. The received account and what it elides. State the thesis flat: the fabric was produced in the gap between the represented and the inhabited city. End on July 2025 — the district dissolved into three wards while this was being written. |
| **2 — The specimen** | **~400** | One named feature, one dated sheet, read closely. Then the 1972 KH-9 frame at 78 cm against it. Then Lefebvre, in one sentence, as the name for what has been shown. Then the self-limiting sentence on reading absence. |
| **3 — Source base + method** | ~230 | The five source types (`cornell-application-plan.md` §16.4) in three sentences, not a list. Then the capabilities, as provenance: MPP, VMA, VnExpress, the preprint, the year lived there. This paragraph must read as *feasible*, which is its whole job. |
| **4 — Fit** | ~230 | Chi, specifically and first. Echols and the 1968 sheet. SEAP and the two-language requirement. Akcan / González Pendás in a clause. Name the training you lack. Close on the argument — not on your career. |

**Order of operations.** Do not draft until you have the specimen. Everything else is assembly; the specimen is the document. Hunt it in the 1882 cadastral first.
