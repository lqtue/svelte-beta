## 3. Why District 4 Is the Right Object

District 4 is a strong dissertation object for reasons that are structural, not sentimental:

- **It is bounded.** A peninsula closed by Kênh Bến Nghé to the north, Kênh Tẻ to the south, and the Saigon River to the east. A closed spatial unit is a gift to morphological analysis across regimes — you can hold the frame constant and let time vary.
- **It carries every regime in one frame.** French port expansion and coolie-labor housing → RVN-era wartime density and Delta in-migration → post-1975 socialist administration → Đổi Mới → present-day tower redevelopment against Thủ Thiêm across the water.
- **It was never planned.** Much of Khánh Hội was marshland into the late 1950s. The alleyway fabric was produced by Mekong Delta migrants occupying and building incrementally. This is Chi's exact research object, in a case she does not have.
- **The administrative unit has just been erased.** In July 2025, Resolution 1685/NQ-UBTVQH15 dissolved District 4 into three wards — Vĩnh Hội, Khánh Hội, Xóm Chiếu. The object of study lost its name during your application year. That is a real "why now," not a manufactured one.

**But "urban evolution of District 4" is not yet a dissertation.** It names a place and a duration. A HAUD committee wants a claim. Proposed reframing:

> **The dissertation argues that District 4's built fabric was produced in the gap between the city that was represented and the city that was inhabited — and that this gap is legible, and measurable, in the cartographic record itself.**

The maps show port infrastructure, wharves, and blank marsh long after the marsh was densely populated. Each regime drew District 4 as what it wanted the district to be: colonial logistics, wartime perimeter, socialist ward, developable riverfront. What was actually built happened in the space the drawings ignored. The dissertation reads the omissions.

This framing does three things at once:
1. It makes VMA both the **evidence base** and the **object of critique** — the maps are sources *and* artifacts of power. That is architectural history, not GIS.
2. It lands directly in Chi's territory: representation, propaganda, city-building, incremental housing.
3. It gives you a claim that can be wrong — which is what separates a proposal from a description.

## 14. Satellite + `greenest-ward` — the Missing "After"

`github.com/lqtue/greenest-ward` (preprint submitted to EarthArXiv) is not a side project. It is the second half of the dissertation's evidence, already built.

Its stated research question — *how do historical political regimes leave detectable patterns in satellite imagery* — classifies HCMC by **historical ring**: pre-1972 core, 1972–76 expansion, 1976–2000, 2000–2025. That is the same regime periodisation as the map corpus, executed in a different sensor. Green cover and land surface temperature run **1990–2025** at five-year steps.

**Use it as the "after" term.** The map corpus stops at 1968 (§13, finding A). Landsat picks up at 1985 and runs to 2025. Together: 1791 → 2025, continuous, on one peninsula. The gap in the paper record is now ~17 years, not 57.

**Do not import the "green debt" framing wholesale.** It is a climate/environmental-policy argument — the same register §4 says to strip out for a history committee. For Cornell the finding is not *"District 4 has a green deficit that policy should address."* It is: *"the divergence between the drawn city and the built city has a measurable present form, and this is it."* Consequence, not recommendation.

**Where the preprint goes:**
- **CV** — yes, prominently. A submitted preprint with national-scale original analysis is the strongest single line on it.
- **SOP** — one clause in Para 4 (evidence and method). It proves you can run quantitative spatial analysis at scale and get it to submission.
- **Writing sample** — **no.** It is a remote-sensing paper. HAUD wants ~20pp of historical argument. Wrong genre; submitting it would read as not understanding the field. Keep the VnExpress adaptation (§6).

### Declassified reconnaissance imagery — the actual gap-closer

Landsat starts in 1985. **Declassified US reconnaissance imagery starts in 1960 and includes a KH-9 HEXAGON frame over Ho Chi Minh City dated 4 November 1972 at 78 cm resolution** (Entity ID `D3C1204-200292F106`).

This is more valuable to the dissertation than Landsat, for four reasons:

1. **It closes the real gap.** The paper corpus ends in 1968. Landsat begins in 1985. KH-9 lands in **1972** — inside the void, at sub-metre resolution.
2. **The date is the right date.** November 1972 is after the Easter Offensive, at the peak of wartime in-migration into Saigon. District 4 at maximum refugee density, photographed from orbit.
3. **78 cm resolves individual roofs.** You can trace the *hẻm* fabric building by building. Landsat at 30 m cannot see an alleyway; KH-9 can see the houses on it. This is footprint-extraction grade — the same work already proven on the 1882 cadastral, on a 1972 frame.
4. **It is free and public domain.** US government work, no licence to clear. Available from **USGS EarthExplorer** at no cost.

## 16. Press, Oral History, and the Year You Lived There

### 16.1 The residency — your strongest and most easily mishandled asset

**You lived in District 4 for over a year.** In the plan as written before this section, that fact appeared nowhere. It should be near the centre.

**What it actually gives you**, none of which is available to a researcher parachuting in:

- You have walked the *hẻm* network. You know which alleys connect and which dead-end — information that appears on no map, which is itself the dissertation's point.
- You know what the ground feels like at 84,000 people per km², which is the difference between reporting a density figure and understanding one.
- You have neighbours, not informants. Introductions come through people who know you.
- You speak the language, read the sources, and are from the city.
- You know the district before and during the redevelopment now reshaping the riverfront.

**How to frame it — and how not to.**

Frame it as **positionality and access**, in one or two sentences, plainly stated. Do not frame it as evidence, do not make it the emotional centre of the academic SOP, and do not let it read as the reason you chose the topic. A history committee reads "I lived there" as a warning sign when it substitutes for method, and as a genuine strength when it sits *next* to method. You have the method — the archive, the georeferencing, the preprint. The residency is what makes the oral-history component credible rather than aspirational.

**Where it goes:**
- **Academic SOP** — one sentence in Para 6 (why you), alongside VMA and the official network. Something close to: *"I lived in District 4 for a year; the fieldwork this project requires begins from inside the neighbourhood rather than from outside it."* Then move on.
- **Personal statement** — **here is where it belongs at length.** This is the document that asks "what's your story?" The year in District 4, what you noticed, and how it turned into a research question is exactly what that document is for. This also resolves an open problem: the Columbia personal statement is a Vietnam→Fulbright→VMA→VnExpress arc, and District 4 gives the Cornell version its own spine.
- **Chi email** — one clause. She works on incremental housing; a researcher who has lived inside an incrementally-built fabric is not a common correspondent.

### 16.2 Oral history — feasible, but check three things

The residency makes interviews realistic. Three items to resolve before promising fieldwork in the SOP:

1. **Cornell IRB.** Under the 2018 revised Common Rule, oral history and historical scholarship are generally excluded from the regulatory definition of "research" with human subjects — but institutions apply this differently. **Confirm with Cornell's IRB how they treat oral history in AAP.** Do not assert either way in the SOP; just describe the method.
2. **Vietnam-side permissions.** Doing interviews as a private resident and doing them as a foreign-university-affiliated doctoral researcher are different activities administratively. Ask Chi and, if possible, a recent Vietnam-fieldwork PhD how they handled it.
3. **Ethics of the actual situation.** You would be interviewing people about homes that redevelopment may take. Anonymisation, consent, and what happens to the recordings need an answer *in the proposal*, not after. A committee will respect the question being raised more than it will respect a clean-sounding plan.

**What the interviews are for, specifically:** the maps show what authority saw; the interviews recover what authority did not record — when a family arrived, from which Delta province, what the ground was when they built on it, how the alley was cut, who filled which pond. That is the *espace vécu* layer in `docs/theory.md:41`, and it is the only source type that can supply it.

### 16.3 Press — the pipeline you already designed

That pipeline was designed for the *Annuaires de l'Indochine*. Newspapers are the same pipeline, and they answer a question the maps cannot: **what was said about the peninsula while it was being built on.**

**Second, the French press is already free and machine-readable.** Gallica material can go straight into the KG pipeline you have already specified. Search it for Khánh Hội, Xóm Chiếu, the port, and the canal works — you will find the administrative language in which the marsh was discussed before it was built on.

### 16.4 What the source base now looks like

The dissertation is no longer a map project with supporting material. It is a five-source-type argument on one peninsula:

| Source type | Coverage | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| Historical maps (VMA, 22 Saigon sheets) | 1791–1968 | How authority **drew** the peninsula |
